Health Burden in Washtenaw County (Ann Arbor), Michigan: Age-Standardized DALY Methodology

Method generation v2 · CDC WONDER 2020–2022 · CDC PLACES 2024 · IHME GBD 2021 · US-2000 standard population · Sergey Soshnikov, MD PhD

This is a planning-grade decision-support estimate, not a peer-reviewed study or an official GBD/IHME county estimate. It is intended to help public-health planners, grant writers, and community stakeholders prioritize. Every input is a public dataset; every step is reproducible.

1. Overview

This analysis quantifies the disease burden of Washtenaw County (which contains Ann Arbor) as Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) across seven condition groups: cancer, cardiovascular disease, chronic lower respiratory disease (COPD), stroke, diabetes, substance-use disorder, and a mental-health group. A DALY is one lost year of healthy life; it is the sum of Years of Life Lost to premature death (YLL) and Years Lived with Disability (YLD).

It uses the v2 age-standardized method, an upgrade over the crude method used for this site's smaller rural counties. The three upgrades: (a) YLL is computed age-specifically from a GBD reference life table rather than against one fixed benchmark age; (b) rates are age-standardized to the US-2000 population so counties with very different age structures can be compared fairly; (c) substance-use and mental-health mortality are directly observed by age, because Washtenaw is large enough that these counts are not statistically suppressed.

2. Data sources

2.1 Mortality — CDC WONDER

Deaths by cause × ten-year age group, Washtenaw County (FIPS 26161), 2020–2022 pooled, from the CDC WONDER Underlying Cause of Death, 2018–2024, Single Race database. The five chronic causes are the aggregate 113-cause-list rows: malignant neoplasms (C00–C97), diseases of heart (I00–I09, I11, I13, I20–I51), chronic lower respiratory (J40–J47), cerebrovascular (I60–I69), and diabetes (E10–E14). Substance use is the sum of drug-induced and alcohol-induced causes; the mental-health mortality proxy is intentional self-harm/suicide (X60–X84).

2.2 Prevalence — CDC PLACES 2024

Age-adjusted adult prevalence for each condition from the CDC PLACES county dataset (Socrata swc5-untb), which also supplies the adult (18+) population denominator. PLACES has no substance-use measure (see §4).

2.3 Disability weights & reference life expectancy

Disability weights are the IHME GBD 2021 values, reused unchanged across all counties (they are population-independent). Age-specific remaining life expectancy for YLL comes from the GBD 2019 reference life table (the same standard IHME uses), interpolated to each age group's midpoint.

2.4 Population denominators — Census PEP via CDC WONDER

Population by county × age group is drawn from the CDC WONDER Single-Race Population Estimates 2020–2022 (the Census Bureau's Population Estimates Program — the same denominators CDC uses for official age-adjusted death rates). We sum 2020, 2021 and 2022 to obtain person-years matching the 3-year pooled deaths, then annualize. Five-year population bands are collapsed to the ten-year mortality bands.

2.5 Economic parameters

Michigan GDP per capita $62,000 (BEA) for the human-capital method; a value of a statistical life of $14.1M for the VSL method (HHS ASPE 2026 central value, 2025 dollars, applied uniformly to all six counties). Through October 2026 this page used $10.5M, the earlier Isabella value. Comorbidity correction factor 0.949 (Gaussian-copula net-YLD correction), reused from the Isabella pipeline.

3. Methods

3.1 DALY framework

DALY = YLL + YLD, no age-weighting and no time discounting, following the WHO Global Health Estimates convention.

3.2 Age-specific YLL

For each cause i and ten-year age group a with midpoint age ma:

YLL(i,a) = deaths(i,a) × RLE(m_a)

where RLE(m_a) is the remaining life expectancy at age ma interpolated from the GBD 2019 reference table. Pooled deaths are annualized (÷3). This replaces the crude method's single fixed 89.1-year benchmark, so a death at 40 and a death at 80 correctly lose different amounts of life.

3.3 YLD

YLD(i) = prevalence_ageadj(i) × adult_pop × DW(i) × CF

Prevalence is the PLACES age-adjusted value; DW the IHME disability weight; CF the 0.949 comorbidity factor. For the mental-health group, prevalence is PLACES depression × 0.50 (remission adjustment). PLACES provides no age-specific prevalence, so YLD carries no age structure (see §4).

3.4 Age-standardization (US-2000)

The cross-county comparison metric is the age-standardized YLL rate: the age-specific YLL rate per 100,000 in each band, weighted by the US-2000 standard population.

ASR_YLL = Σ_a [ YLL(a) / pop(a) × 100000 ] × w2000(a)

Standard weights are normalized over the ten age bands present (the <1 band is folded into 1–4 to match the population banding, and weights re-sum to 1). Washtenaw's age-standardized YLL rate is 8,251 per 100,000.

3.5 Substance-use & mental-health mortality

Unlike small rural counties, Washtenaw's substance-use and suicide deaths are numerous enough to be reported by age rather than suppressed, so both are observed directly rather than modeled from proxies.

3.6 Suppression handling

CDC WONDER suppresses cell counts of 1–9. Suppressed chronic-disease cells at young ages (<45), where the true count is near zero, are imputed at 1; suppressed substance-use/suicide cells are imputed at the midpoint 5. A 0-to-9 sensitivity band brackets the choice; the swing on the county total is under ±2%.

3.7 Economic burden

Human-capital: DALYs × $62,000. VSL: annual deaths × $14.1M (mortality only). Both are planning-grade, not audited fiscal figures.

4. Limitations & comparability

Comparability with this site's rural counties. Washtenaw and Wayne use the v2 age-standardized method; Isabella, Clare, Midland and Gratiot use the earlier v1 crude method (fixed reference life expectancy; substance-use and mental-health mortality suppressed and excluded). Rates across the two groups are not directly comparable and must not be ranked against one another. Compare within a method group only, or see the dedicated Rural vs Urban page, which discusses the seam explicitly.
  • "Age-standardized" applies to YLL only. Because PLACES gives no age-specific prevalence, YLD is reported as a crude rate (7,059/100k) and shown separately rather than blended into a single "age-standardized DALY rate."
  • Substance-use YLD prevalence is indicative. With no PLACES SUD measure, the SUD disability component uses a Michigan-derived rate applied uniformly; it does not reflect local variation. SUD mortality (YLL) is directly observed.
  • SUD/suicide overlap. Drug-poisoning suicides (X60–X64) fall in both the substance-use and suicide categories, so the sum of those two causes' YLL is a modest upper bound.
  • Prevalence basis. An age-adjusted prevalence is multiplied by an actual adult count — a standard but internally mixed convention.
  • Geography. Mortality is county-resolution only; Ann Arbor is described as part of Washtenaw County.

v4 Methodology update: age-specific YLL and unified economics (November 2026)

Version 4 puts all six Lakes Linked counties on one basis. Washtenaw County was already computed with age-specific YLL (v2, July 2026); the burden totals are unchanged in v4. What changed is the economic layer: a single VSL formula is now applied to every county, replacing the $10.5M placeholder carried over from the Isabella analysis.

Years of life lost. YLL = Σa deathsa × RLE(a). Deaths by ten-year age group are taken from CDC WONDER Underlying Cause of Death 2020–2022 (113 Cause List; single-race population files) and annualized by dividing by three. RLE(a) is remaining life expectancy at the age-band midpoint from the GBD 2019 reference life table (life expectancy at birth 88.87 years; doi:10.6069/1D4Y-YQ37). This replaces the v1 planning approximation deaths × (terminal age − mean age at death).

Denominators and rates. Population is CDC WONDER Single-Race Population Estimates 2020–2022 (dataset D191) expressed as person-years per year: 367,730. The age-standardized YLL rate uses the US 2000 standard population with the under-1 band folded into 1–4: 8,251 per 100,000. The crude DALY rate is 15,943 per 100,000.

Years lived with disability are unchanged from the earlier version: CDC PLACES prevalence, IHME GBD 2021 disability weights at moderate severity, mental-health remission factor 0.50, and the comorbidity correction factor 0.949.

Economics. Human capital = DALYs × $62,000 (Michigan GDP per capita, BEA 2024). VSL: $14.1M per statistical life (HHS ASPE 2026 central value, 2025 dollars), applied uniformly to every county. HHS adjusts VSL for income over time but not across places, so no county-level income adjustment is used; an income-adjusted variant is reported as a sensitivity analysis in the methods supplement. Human capital and VSL are alternative valuations and are never added.

v4.1 Methodology update: one YLD rule for all six counties (11 November 2026)

v4 put YLL for all six counties on one basis. A check of the inputs then showed that YLD had not been: each county carried its own prevalence inputs, some crude and some age-adjusted, from different CDC PLACES releases, with the mental-health remission adjustment applied in some counties only and cardiovascular disease taken from hypertension prevalence in Washtenaw and Wayne. v4.1 applies one rule everywhere.

  • Source. CDC PLACES 2024 release (BRFSS 2023), adults 18+, the same release for all six counties.
  • Counts. YLD = crude prevalence × adults 18+ (CDC WONDER D191, 2020–2022 mean) × disability weight (IHME GBD 2021) × comorbidity factor 0.949.
  • Rates. Age-standardized YLD = age-adjusted prevalence × disability weight × 0.949 × the 18+ share of the US 2000 standard population (0.742). Added to age-standardized YLL, this gives an age-standardized DALY rate comparable across counties.
  • Condition definitions. Cardiovascular disease: PLACES coronary heart disease. Mental health: PLACES depression × 0.50 remission, in every county. Substance use: PLACES has no measure; the Isabella derived prevalence (7.2%) is scaled by each county's binge-drinking prevalence relative to Isabella's. This is the weakest input and is flagged as such.
  • Uncertainty. 95% uncertainty intervals from 4,000 draws over the PLACES 95% confidence limits, substance-use prevalence ×0.7–1.3, and the CDC WONDER suppression range for YLL. Disability weights are not varied, so the intervals are partial.

Scope. The estimates cover seven condition groups: cancer (ICD-10 C00–C97); cardiovascular disease, diseases of the heart (I00–I09, I11, I13, I20–I51); stroke (I60–I69); COPD and chronic lower respiratory disease (J40–J47); diabetes (E10–E14); mental health conditions (F30–F48 for disability, suicide X60–X84 for mortality); and substance use disorders (F10–F19, drug- and alcohol-induced deaths). YLL counts deaths at all ages. YLD counts adults 18+ only, because CDC PLACES surveys adults; disability in children is not counted, so the YLD part of each rate is a lower bound. Other causes, including injuries, COVID-19, Alzheimer's disease, kidney and liver disease, are not yet included. The next version will extend the analysis to all causes, with all-cause YLL as the anchor and additional PLACES conditions (asthma, arthritis, chronic kidney disease) for YLD; risk factors such as obesity, hypertension and smoking will not be added as conditions, to avoid double counting.

Result for Washtenaw County. 58,628 DALYs/yr (YLL 32,670 + YLD 25,958; 95% UI 56,502–62,162), against 63,379 in v4 (YLD 30,709). Crude rate 15,943 per 100,000; age-standardized rate 14,708 per 100,000 (95% UI 14,172–15,676). Cardiovascular YLD falls from 5,638 to 997 because the earlier input was hypertension prevalence (28.3%), a risk factor rather than a disease, where the other counties used heart disease. v4.1 uses PLACES coronary heart disease for all six. Counts now use crude rather than age-adjusted prevalence. Substance use moves to #2 and cardiovascular disease to #3.

Remaining limitations: prevalence (BRFSS 2023) and deaths (2020–2022) cover different years; YLD under age 18 is not counted; the remission factor and comorbidity factor are carried over from the Isabella analysis rather than estimated locally; for Clare, Gratiot and Midland, suicide deaths are suppressed and modeled from Isabella's observed rate. Results file results_v41_2026-11-11.json, script run_v41.py.

6. Reproducibility & verification

The pipeline (Python) parses the raw CDC WONDER exports, applies the formulas above, and writes a machine-readable results file. An independent agent re-parsed the raw death files and re-implemented the engine from scratch, reproducing all 56 cause × county × metric cells and every total to the integer. Two suppression-sensitivity runs (0 and 9) bracket the central estimate. Data vintages: CDC WONDER 2020–2022, CDC PLACES 2024, CDC Single-Race Population Estimates 2020–2022, IHME GBD 2021.

Scope: seven condition groups (cancer; cardiovascular disease (diseases of the heart); stroke; COPD and chronic lower respiratory disease; diabetes; mental health conditions; substance use disorders). YLL: all ages. YLD: adults 18+ only (CDC PLACES); disability in children is not counted. Expansion to all causes in preparation.
