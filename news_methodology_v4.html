How we rebuilt the Lakes Linked methodology (v4, November 2026)

News · Methods · Sergey Soshnikov, MD PhD · 11 November 2026

Lakes Linked started as a planning tool: a fast, transparent way to put disease burden for a Michigan county on one page, using only public data. In November 2026 we rebuilt the engine underneath it. All six counties on the site now run on one method, one set of denominators and one value for the economic burden of a death. This note explains what changed, how the numbers moved, what we checked, and what is still uncertain.

Years lived with disability are computed as before: CDC PLACES prevalence, IHME GBD 2021 disability weights, a mental-health remission factor of 0.50 and a comorbidity correction factor of 0.949. Both factors were derived for Isabella County and are applied to the other five counties as an assumption.

What we checked

Against an external source

The clearest external check comes from IHME's modelled county estimates. IHME puts Clare County's all-cause mortality at 1,014 per 100,000, about 30% above the Michigan average of 783. The v1 model had assigned Clare the state average. The higher observed death count in v4 moves Clare in the direction IHME independently indicates.

Against the published result

For Isabella, holding death totals fixed and changing only the YLL method moves total years of life lost by 38 years (0.5%). The full v4 estimate, which also uses observed deaths by age, is 1.3% below the published figure and well inside its uncertainty interval. A method change that moved the headline a lot would have been a warning sign; this one did not.

For internal consistency

  • The population files pulled for this update reproduced the earlier Washtenaw and Wayne denominators exactly, cell for cell. This confirms the data pull, not the estimates.
  • For Isabella, the old state-rate proxy for substance-use deaths (about 28 a year) and the v4 count (26.7 a year) agree within 5%. The v4 count includes suppressed cells we imputed, so this comparison is not fully independent.
  • Gratiot's v1 analysis projected about 10,700 DALYs on the frontier standard; v4 gives 10,475. Both come from our own pipeline.

Condition by condition

In Isabella, the old shortcut understated years of life lost from COPD (by about 21%) and diabetes (about 24%) and overstated stroke (about 9%), because deaths in these groups are spread across ages in ways a single average cannot capture. We have not yet run the same comparison for the other counties.

What we learned

Nonmetro Clare and metro Detroit carry comparable age-standardized mortality burden: 14,472 against 14,177 years of life lost per 100,000. The 2% gap is too small to rank them.

Below the top two, the metro line holds. All three nonmetro counties (Clare, Isabella, Gratiot) sit above the two smaller, higher-income metro counties, Midland and Washtenaw. Detroit is the exception among metro counties. Isabella and Gratiot (12,817 and 12,457) cannot be told apart without uncertainty intervals.

Clare's priorities look different on observed data. Substance use moves from first to fourth by DALYs: cancer, cardiovascular disease and COPD carry more total burden. Opioid deaths still occur at the youngest ages of any condition, so substance use remains the leading target for acute intervention.

The economic burden of mortality, valued the same way everywhere, ranges from $4.7B a year in Clare to $180B in Wayne County, in proportion to deaths alone.

Limits we want readers to keep in mind

  • Pandemic years. Deaths are from 2020–2022. Excess mortality from cardiovascular disease, diabetes and other causes during COVID-19 is included in these counts, while COVID-19 itself is outside the seven condition groups. Rates may be higher than in a typical period.
  • No uncertainty intervals yet for v4. All six-county comparisons are point estimates. Only Isabella has published intervals.
  • Imputation is not neutral. Setting suppressed chronic-disease cells under 45 to 1 adds a small amount of YLL. The suicide age shape is borrowed from Washtenaw, an urban college county, and may not match rural age patterns.
  • Cause definition. The COPD group is labelled J44 but the mortality query covered chronic lower respiratory diseases (J40–J47), which includes asthma and bronchiectasis.
  • Disability burden relies on modelled adult prevalence from CDC PLACES, and the remission and comorbidity factors were estimated for one county.
  • These are planning-grade estimates from public data, not official GBD county estimates.

What is next

  • uncertainty intervals for every county on the v4 basis;
  • calibration of the age pattern against IHME county mortality estimates, with CDC WONDER kept as the observed backbone;
  • a sensitivity run excluding 2020–2021, once 2023–2024 county data can be pooled;
  • Mecosta County, the next county in the pipeline.
