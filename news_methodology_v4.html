Lakes Linked v4.1: one method for six Michigan counties

News · Methods update · Sergey Soshnikov, MD PhD · 11 November 2026

Lakes Linked now estimates disease burden for six Michigan counties with a single method. Clare, Gratiot, Isabella, Midland, Washtenaw and Wayne counties are all calculated from observed deaths by age, the standard GBD formula for years of life lost, one rule for years lived with disability, the same population denominators and the same value per statistical life. For the first time, the six counties can be compared directly.

The update changes some headline numbers and one county's priority ranking. It also makes every estimate on the site easier to check. Below is a short account of what we changed, what the new figures show, how we tested them, and where the limits are.

What is new

  • Observed deaths, not adjusted state rates. Each county's deaths now come from its own death records (CDC WONDER, 2020 to 2022), broken down by ten-year age group. Earlier versions estimated some counties from Michigan state rates with a rural adjustment.
  • Standard years of life lost. Each death is weighted by the remaining life expectancy at that age, using the GBD 2019 reference life table. This replaces a shortcut that assumed all deaths in a cause group happened at the group's average age.
  • Comparable rates. Every county now has an age-standardized rate, so a retirement-age county, a college town and a large city can be compared fairly.
  • One value per statistical life. All counties use $14.1M, the current federal (HHS) central value. Earlier pages used five different values. We no longer lower the value for lower-income counties.
  • One rule for disability. Years lived with disability now come from the same CDC PLACES release for every county, with the same condition definitions and the same mental-health remission adjustment. Before this, the counties had inherited different inputs, which moved some conditions by a factor of two to four (update of 11 November 2026, v4.1).
  • Uncertainty ranges. Every county now has a 95% range for its total and its age-standardized rate.
  • Tables that add up. Every row and column on the site now sums to its stated total, and every county page carries a version mark.

What the new figures show

Rural Clare and Detroit carry about the same burden. After adjusting for age, Clare County loses 22,122 healthy years per 100,000 residents each year (95% range 19,818 to 23,941) and Wayne County (Detroit) loses 20,991 (20,474 to 21,608). The ranges overlap, so neither can be called worse. Mortality alone gives the same picture: 14,472 and 14,177 years of life lost per 100,000.

Smaller metro counties do better. All three nonmetropolitan counties (Clare, Isabella, Gratiot) have higher age-standardized burden than Midland and Washtenaw, the two smaller and higher-income metropolitan counties. Detroit is the exception among metro counties.

Clare's ranking changed. With observed deaths, cancer, heart disease and COPD carry more total burden in Clare than substance use, which moves from first to fourth. Opioid deaths still happen at younger ages than any other cause, so substance use remains the most urgent target for immediate intervention.

The Isabella estimate holds. The new method puts Isabella County at 13,089 DALYs a year (95% range 12,268 to 13,787), 3.0% below the 13,493 published in Health Research and Education Innovation in 2026 and well within that study's uncertainty range. The site continues to show the published figure as the headline.

How we checked the results

An outside source agrees with the direction of change. The Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) estimates Clare County's all-cause death rate at 1,014 per 100,000, about 30% above the Michigan average of 783. Our earlier model had given Clare the state average. The higher death count in v4 moves Clare toward the IHME figure.

The new method changes little where it should change little. For Isabella, switching only the YLL formula, with deaths held fixed, moved the total by 0.5%.

Internal checks are consistent. Population files reproduced earlier ones exactly, and Isabella's substance-use deaths match the earlier estimate within 5%. These checks confirm that the pipeline works; they are not independent evidence.

Individual conditions shifted more than totals. In Isabella, the old shortcut understated years of life lost from COPD by about 21% and from diabetes by about 24%, and overstated stroke by about 9%. We have not yet repeated this comparison for the other counties.

What to keep in mind

  • Seven conditions, not all causes. The estimates cover cancer, cardiovascular disease, stroke, COPD, diabetes, mental health conditions and substance use disorders. Injuries, COVID-19, Alzheimer's disease, kidney and liver disease and other causes are not yet included, so totals show part of the full burden.
  • Adults only for disability. Years of life lost count deaths at all ages. Years lived with disability count adults 18 and older only, because the CDC PLACES survey covers adults; disability in children is not counted.
  • Pandemic years. The death data cover 2020 to 2022, when deaths from heart disease, diabetes and other causes rose during COVID-19. COVID-19 itself is not one of the seven conditions.
  • Partial uncertainty ranges. The ranges cover survey prevalence, withheld death counts and substance-use prevalence, but not disability weights. Overlapping results, such as Clare, Wayne, Isabella and Gratiot, cannot be ranked against each other.
  • Substance use. CDC PLACES does not measure substance use disorders, so their prevalence is modeled from one county's estimate and local binge drinking. This is the weakest input.
  • Small numbers. In small counties, some death counts are withheld for privacy. We fill these gaps with stated rules, including a suicide age pattern borrowed from Washtenaw County, and these choices add uncertainty.
  • Definitions. The COPD group includes all chronic lower respiratory deaths (ICD-10 J40 to J47). Disability estimates rely on modelled adult prevalence and on two adjustment factors first estimated for Isabella County.
  • These are planning estimates built from public data. They are not official GBD county estimates.

What comes next

  • Extending the analysis from seven conditions to all causes
  • Disability-weight uncertainty in the ranges, and a direct county-level source for substance use
  • Calibration against IHME county mortality estimates
  • A check that excludes the 2020 and 2021 pandemic years once newer county data are available
  • Mecosta County, the next county in the series
