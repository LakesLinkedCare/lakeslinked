Rural vs Urban: Michigan County Disease Burden

Six counties on one basis · three nonmetro, three metro (OMB 2023) · v4.1, November 2026

How to read this page. All six counties are on one method (v4.1, 11 November 2026). YLL: age-specific, summed on the GBD 2019 reference life table, from observed CDC WONDER 2020–22 deaths by ten-year age group. YLD: one rule for every county, from the CDC PLACES 2024 release. Counties are classified by the OMB 2023 delineation: Clare, Isabella and Gratiot are nonmetropolitan; Midland (its own metropolitan statistical area), Washtenaw and Wayne are metropolitan. The column to read is age-standardized DALYs per 100,000 (US 2000 standard population): it removes the age-structure difference between a retirement-age nonmetro county, a college town and a large city. Crude rates are shown alongside because they are what a county actually experiences. Mortality for substance use and mental health is suppressed in small counties; those cells are reconstructed as stated on each county's methods page.
Scope: seven condition groups. These DALYs cover cancer; cardiovascular disease (diseases of the heart); stroke; COPD and chronic lower respiratory disease; diabetes; mental health conditions; substance use disorders. YLL: all ages. YLD: adults 18+ only (CDC PLACES surveys adults), so disability in children is not counted. Other causes, including injuries, COVID-19, Alzheimer's disease, kidney and liver disease, are not yet included, so totals show part of the full burden. Expansion to all causes is in preparation.

On one basis, the spread across six counties is about 1.50×, from Clare at 22,122 age-standardized DALYs per 100,000 down to Washtenaw at 14,708. The result worth stating carefully is at the top: Clare, a nonmetro county of 31,000, and Wayne County (Detroit), with 1.77 million, have overlapping uncertainty intervals (19,818–23,941 and 20,474–21,608), so the honest reading is that they are comparable, not that Clare is worse than Detroit. Isabella and Gratiot also overlap with each other and with Wayne. Below them the metro line holds: Midland and Washtenaw, both higher-income metro counties, have the lowest rates and intervals that do not reach any nonmetro county. Detroit is the exception among metro counties, and Clare matches it. Crude rates tell a different story for a reason: Clare leads on crude burden (30,982 per 100,000) partly because its population is old, which standardization removes. The intervals are partial (disability weights are not varied) and the substance-use prevalence is a modeled input in every county.

Different cities, different burden signatures

What the cause mix says about each place

Ann Arbor — cancer-led, healthiest

A young, highly educated, well-insured population where chronic disease (cancer, then CVD) dominates and the overall age-standardized burden is the lowest of the analyzed counties.

Detroit — cardiovascular-led, heaviest

Heart disease is a third of the burden, with an enormous substance-use component (1,107 deaths/yr). Deaths also strike younger, pushing the age-standardized DALY rate to 1.4× Ann Arbor's.

Substance use, now visible

Substance use is directly observed in both cities: the #2 driver in Washtenaw and #3 in Wayne. In small rural counties these deaths are statistically suppressed and effectively invisible — a key blind spot the urban data exposes.
