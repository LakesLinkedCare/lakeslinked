Health Burden from NCDs, SUDs, and MHCs in Midland County, Michigan: Planning-Grade DALY Estimates

Cross-sectional analysis for community health planning, 2026
Sergey Soshnikov, MD PhD
Lakes Linked · Assistant Professor, Public Health · Lakes Linked · contact@lakeslinked.com
Version 1.0 June 2026 Not peer-reviewed Planning purposes only WHO GHE Frontier LE 89.1 yrs (primary) Michigan LE 78.6 yrs (planning sensitivity) Prevalence: MI state proxies ⚠️ FIPS 26111
Abstract

Background. Midland County, Michigan (pop. 82,884) is an industrial economy county anchored by Dow Chemical Company global headquarters. Unlike rural neighbors Clare and Isabella, Midland has higher median household income ($62,000), lower poverty (9%), and a more suburban–industrial SDOH profile. This analysis applies the Lakes Linked DALY framework — which uses disability weights and WHO reference standards from the IHME Global Burden of Disease Study, a body of work we deeply respect — as an educational planning tool to estimate health burden as a baseline for grant applications and county health planning. This is not a formal GBD estimate and does not attempt to replicate IHME's rigorous global methodology.

Methods. DALY estimates were constructed from CDC PLACES 2023 (BRFSS 2021–22, age-adjusted county-level prevalence for FIPS 26111), CDC WONDER 2020–22 (pooled 3-year mortality counts), IHME GBD 2021 disability weights, and U.S. Census ACS 2022 population denominators. YLL uses the WHO GHE frontier reference life table (89.1 years) as primary standard; Michigan observed LE (78.6 years) is the planning sensitivity. MH remission factor ×0.50 applied. Pipeline: director.py --county midland --steps harvest,calculate,factcheck.

Results (v4.1, November 2026). Primary burden, age-specific YLL summed on the GBD 2019 reference life table: 17,217 DALYs/yr (YLL 10,477 + YLD 6,740). Cancer leads (5,606 DALYs), followed by CVD (3,111) and Mental Health (1,737). Observed deaths (CDC WONDER 2020–22, seven cause groups): 560/yr, against 592/yr estimated in v1. Economic burden: ~$1.07B/yr (human capital). The v1 Michigan-LE figures (14,335 DALYs; YLL 6,505 + YLD 9,332; 592 deaths) are retained in the table below.

Limitations. SUD prevalence derived from binge-drinking + OUD proxy (direct SUD county prevalence not available in CDC PLACES). Industrial occupational exposures (Dow Chemical PFAS legacy, chemical manufacturing) are not captured in standard DALY frameworks and may result in underestimation of COPD and cancer burden in the Midland workforce.

Keywords: disability-adjusted life years · disease burden · Michigan · Midland County · industrial health · Dow Chemical · DALY methodology · public health planning · CDC PLACES 2023

1. Data Availability and Quality

1.1 CDC PLACES API Status

The primary source for YLD prevalence inputs — CDC PLACES 2024 (BRFSS 2021–22, MLRP model) — was queried via the Socrata API endpoint (data.cdc.gov/resource/swc5-untb.json) with filter locationid=26111 during the June 2026 pipeline run. The API returned 0 records. This may reflect a temporary API proxy issue, a data release delay for Midland County, or a structural endpoint change. CDC PLACES does publish Midland County estimates in its full release files; the issue is API access, not data availability.

1.2 Mortality Data (CDC WONDER)

CDC WONDER 3-year pooled mortality (2020–2022) was queried for FIPS 26111. Midland County has a larger population than Clare (82,884 vs 30,013), which reduces but does not eliminate suppression. Some cause-specific death counts for lower-mortality conditions (Stroke, Diabetes) may still reflect state-rate substitution with rural adjustment. The pipeline harvester.py applies rural adjustment factors where suppression is detected.

3. Industrial and Environmental Health Context

Midland County is home to Dow Chemical Company global headquarters and a large industrial manufacturing base. This creates SDOH and occupational health considerations not captured in the standard DALY framework:

3.1 PFAS Legacy Contamination

Midland County has documented legacy contamination from dioxin and PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) associated with Dow Chemical historical operations. PFAS exposure is associated with thyroid disease, kidney cancer, ulcerative colitis, and testicular cancer — conditions that may elevate cancer burden beyond what Michigan state-rate proxies would predict. This analysis does not adjust for PFAS exposure due to insufficient county-specific dose-response data at the time of analysis.

3.2 Occupational Health Burden

Industrial chemical manufacturing workforces have elevated rates of occupational lung disease, hearing loss, and chemical exposure-related cancers — none of which are fully captured in CDC PLACES or standard DALY frameworks. The directly observed COPD prevalence (6.3%, CDC PLACES 2023) is below the Michigan state average, consistent with Midland's higher income and better healthcare access relative to rural peers. Conversely, the higher income and better healthcare access may mitigate burden relative to the state average.

3.3 Net Assessment

The direction of bias in current estimates is uncertain: industrial exposures push burden higher; higher income and better access push it lower. Until direct CDC PLACES data and environmental health data can be incorporated, ±20–30% uncertainty should be applied to all per-condition estimates for Midland County.

v4 Methodology update: age-specific YLL and unified economics (November 2026)

Version 4 puts all six Lakes Linked counties on one basis. For Midland County this replaced the v1 planning estimate of 14,335 (Michigan LE, mean-age YLL) with the results below.

Years of life lost. YLL = Σa deathsa × RLE(a). Deaths by ten-year age group are taken from CDC WONDER Underlying Cause of Death 2020–2022 (113 Cause List; single-race population files) and annualized by dividing by three. RLE(a) is remaining life expectancy at the age-band midpoint from the GBD 2019 reference life table (life expectancy at birth 88.87 years; doi:10.6069/1D4Y-YQ37). This replaces the v1 planning approximation deaths × (terminal age − mean age at death).

Denominators and rates. Population is CDC WONDER Single-Race Population Estimates 2020–2022 (dataset D191) expressed as person-years per year: 83,527. The age-standardized YLL rate uses the US 2000 standard population with the under-1 band folded into 1–4: 9,578 per 100,000. The crude DALY rate is 20,612 per 100,000.

Suppression handling. CDC WONDER suppresses cells with 1–9 deaths. Suicide (the mortality component of mental health) is suppressed in every age band for this county, so the known three-year county total is distributed across age bands using the Washtenaw County age shape as a donor. Drug- and alcohol-induced deaths (substance use) are partly suppressed; suppressed cells are imputed at the midpoint 5 with a 0–9 band carried into the uncertainty range. Chronic-disease cells suppressed below age 45 are imputed at 1. The rural-adjustment multipliers of v1 are no longer used anywhere.

Years lived with disability are unchanged from the earlier version: CDC PLACES prevalence, IHME GBD 2021 disability weights at moderate severity, mental-health remission factor 0.50, and the comorbidity correction factor 0.949.

Economics. Human capital = DALYs × $62,000 (Michigan GDP per capita, BEA 2024). VSL: $14.1M per statistical life (HHS ASPE 2026 central value, 2025 dollars), applied uniformly to every county. HHS adjusts VSL for income over time but not across places, so no county-level income adjustment is used; an income-adjusted variant is reported as a sensitivity analysis in the methods supplement. Human capital and VSL are alternative valuations and are never added.

v4.1 Methodology update: one YLD rule for all six counties (11 November 2026)

v4 put YLL for all six counties on one basis. A check of the inputs then showed that YLD had not been: each county carried its own prevalence inputs, some crude and some age-adjusted, from different CDC PLACES releases, with the mental-health remission adjustment applied in some counties only and cardiovascular disease taken from hypertension prevalence in Washtenaw and Wayne. v4.1 applies one rule everywhere.

  • Source. CDC PLACES 2024 release (BRFSS 2023), adults 18+, the same release for all six counties.
  • Counts. YLD = crude prevalence × adults 18+ (CDC WONDER D191, 2020–2022 mean) × disability weight (IHME GBD 2021) × comorbidity factor 0.949.
  • Rates. Age-standardized YLD = age-adjusted prevalence × disability weight × 0.949 × the 18+ share of the US 2000 standard population (0.742). Added to age-standardized YLL, this gives an age-standardized DALY rate comparable across counties.
  • Condition definitions. Cardiovascular disease: PLACES coronary heart disease. Mental health: PLACES depression × 0.50 remission, in every county. Substance use: PLACES has no measure; the Isabella derived prevalence (7.2%) is scaled by each county's binge-drinking prevalence relative to Isabella's. This is the weakest input and is flagged as such.
  • Uncertainty. 95% uncertainty intervals from 4,000 draws over the PLACES 95% confidence limits, substance-use prevalence ×0.7–1.3, and the CDC WONDER suppression range for YLL. Disability weights are not varied, so the intervals are partial.

Scope. The estimates cover seven condition groups: cancer (ICD-10 C00–C97); cardiovascular disease, diseases of the heart (I00–I09, I11, I13, I20–I51); stroke (I60–I69); COPD and chronic lower respiratory disease (J40–J47); diabetes (E10–E14); mental health conditions (F30–F48 for disability, suicide X60–X84 for mortality); and substance use disorders (F10–F19, drug- and alcohol-induced deaths). YLL counts deaths at all ages. YLD counts adults 18+ only, because CDC PLACES surveys adults; disability in children is not counted, so the YLD part of each rate is a lower bound. Other causes, including injuries, COVID-19, Alzheimer's disease, kidney and liver disease, are not yet included. The next version will extend the analysis to all causes, with all-cause YLL as the anchor and additional PLACES conditions (asthma, arthritis, chronic kidney disease) for YLD; risk factors such as obesity, hypertension and smoking will not be added as conditions, to avoid double counting.

Result for Midland County. 17,217 DALYs/yr (YLL 10,477 + YLD 6,740; 95% UI 16,323–18,014), against 17,907 in v4 (YLD 7,430). Crude rate 20,612 per 100,000; age-standardized rate 16,405 per 100,000 (95% UI 15,358–17,364). Mental-health YLD falls from 2,513 to 1,182 because the 0.50 remission adjustment, applied in Isabella, had not been applied here. Cancer YLD rises from 1,442 to 1,851 because the earlier input was the age-adjusted 7.7%, while a count needs the crude 10.0%. Mental health moves from #3 to #5; substance use is #3 and COPD #4.

Remaining limitations: prevalence (BRFSS 2023) and deaths (2020–2022) cover different years; YLD under age 18 is not counted; the remission factor and comorbidity factor are carried over from the Isabella analysis rather than estimated locally; for Clare, Gratiot and Midland, suicide deaths are suppressed and modeled from Isabella's observed rate. Results file results_v41_2026-11-11.json, script run_v41.py.

5. Path to Full Analysis

Upgrading this analysis from planning-grade to CHNA-ready requires the following steps:

  1. Download CDC PLACES 2024 full county CSV from data.cdc.gov and filter FIPS 26111
  2. Re-run: python3 director.py --county midland --steps harvest,calculate,factcheck
  3. Verify direct CDC WONDER 2020–22 counts for all 7 condition groups; reduce reliance on state-rate substitution
  4. Consult Midland County CHR 2024 for local-specific SDOH measures
  5. Consider PFAS-adjusted cancer burden analysis using MDHHS environmental health data
  6. Update midland_county_config.json and regenerate midland.html dashboard
